    How it works

    1. Palette. Find the background and the dominant colours; label every pixel, ignoring dithering and antialiasing blends.
    2. Objects. Connected regions per colour, linked across frames, with pieces cut by an occluder joined back together.
    3. Motion. Each object's clearest view becomes a template, fitted on every frame for position, rotation and scale; pixels hidden by another object are not counted against it.
    4. Keyframes. Per-frame values are reduced to keyframes (Ramer–Douglas–Peucker), which is what you edit and what the exports play.

    On a synthetic benchmark with known sources (3–5 overlapping shapes, dithered GIFs), objects keep their identity on 98.6 % of frames, positions are within 0.14 px and rotations within 0.5° (median). Near-identical colours are still out of reach. Method and numbers.
