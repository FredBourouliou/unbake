## Design Context

### Users
Developers and designers arriving from Hacker News, Reddit or a GitHub link, on a desktop browser, with no idea who built this. They give it ten seconds: drop a GIF (or click a sample), see it come apart into objects, drag a motion path, export. Some are motion designers used to After Effects / Rive; most are curious engineers.

### Brand Personality
Precise, candid, a little playful. Like a well-made instrument: it shows its measurements (frame numbers, keyframe counts, timing) and never oversells. The "reveal" — flat pixels turning into selectable objects — is the one moment of delight.

### Aesthetic Direction
Workshop / darkroom: warm graphite neutrals so the animation colours carry the page, one accent (vermilion) reserved for selection, motion paths and keyframe handles, as in motion-design tools. Numeric readouts in a monospaced face because they are numbers, not decoration. No glow, no gradients, no glass, no cards inside cards.

### Design Principles
1. The canvas is the hero; chrome stays quiet.
2. Show, then let them touch: samples load instantly, everything is draggable.
3. Honest status: say what runs where (Python in your browser, nothing uploaded) and how long it took.
4. Every edit is reversible (undo) and exportable.
5. Keyboard and screen-reader basics: focus states, labels, space = play.
